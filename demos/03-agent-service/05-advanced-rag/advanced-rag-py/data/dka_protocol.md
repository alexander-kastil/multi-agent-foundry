# Management of Diabetic Ketoacidosis

## Overview
Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) is a medical emergency in people with diabetes defined by hyperglycaemia, ketonaemia and acidosis. Treatment priorities are fluids, insulin, electrolytes and finding the precipitating cause. Admit to a monitored area and review every hour.

## Fluid resuscitation
Give 1 litre of 0.9 percent sodium chloride over the first hour. Continue 0.9 percent sodium chloride at 250 to 500 mL per hour guided by volume status. Add dextrose 5 percent once glucose falls below 14 mmol/L.

## Insulin therapy
Start a fixed-rate intravenous insulin infusion at 0.1 units per kg per hour. Continue basal subcutaneous insulin alongside the infusion. Do not stop the infusion until ketones are below 0.6 mmol/L and the patient is eating.

## Potassium replacement
Check serum potassium before starting insulin. If potassium is above 5.3 mmol/L give none and recheck in two hours. If it is 3.3 to 5.3 mmol/L add 40 mmol per litre of fluid. If it is below 3.3 mmol/L hold insulin and give potassium first.

## Complication management
Watch for cerebral oedema in younger patients, hypoglycaemia and hypokalaemia during treatment, and fluid overload in heart failure. Escalate to critical care when pH stays below 7.0 after the first hour.
